I poured ink onto a sheet of damp paper last month and watched it find its way
to the edge. Then I went back to my desk and ran flow accumulation over a
filled DEM, and watched the same shape appear.

<!--more-->

That isn't a metaphor I was looking for. It's just what happened.

## Two ways of asking where water goes

The model is an argument made out of rules. Every cell drains to its lowest
neighbour; do that a few million times and the drainage lines emerge because
they *have* to. It's completely determined, and it's still surprising, because
nobody can hold a few million steps in their head.

The paper is an argument made out of materials. The ink goes where the damp is,
the damp goes where the fibre is, and I get about four seconds of influence
before the sheet takes over.

Both are ways of asking the landscape a question and letting it answer.

## What each one is bad at

The model can't tell you what it feels like to be downstream. It gives you a
travel time in hours and a confidence you probably shouldn't have.

The painting can't tell you when to leave. It's not for that.
